# Code of Conduct

This project is about queer people's safety. It is not a debate club, and we are not neutral
about whether people deserve the rights we document. Everything below follows from that.

**Where it applies:** this repository, its issues, discussions and pull requests, the project's
Discord if you got there from here, and any space where you are representing rights.wtf.
It also applies outside those spaces when conduct there affects someone's ability to participate
safely here.

---

## What we want

- Assume the person asking the question is new and scared. Answer the actual question.
- People's words for themselves are the correct words. Use them. If we get someone's terminology
  wrong, we fix it.
- Say "I don't know" and "as of the date on that page". Uncertainty stated is useful; uncertainty
  hidden is how people get hurt.
- Disagreement about sources, wording, or priority is welcome and usually productive.

## What gets you removed

- **Outing anyone.** Publishing or encouraging disclosure of somebody's identity, name, medical
  status, or history — including your own project contributions that quietly quote a court ruling
  with a full name in it. Also: "researching" who contributors are.
- **Deadnaming and misgendering deliberately**, including in "correcting" someone or in quoting
  material for no reason.
- **Denying that the people in question exist.** Bad-faith biological determinism, "gender is a
  euphemism" reposts, and the current dog-whistle of the month are not technical feedback.
- **Safety-critical misinformation presented as fact.** Telling someone a treatment is safe, that
  a law protects them where it does not, or that a helpline is fine when it is not — this is a
  conduct issue even when it's sincerely and politely written. Use the correction form with sources.
- **Medical or legal advice.** We are a field guide, not a clinician or your lawyer.
- Harassment, stalking, doxxing, spam, sustained off-topic argument, or moderation tourism
  ("report this to the foundation instead of arguing here").
- Using this project's reach to promote a personal service, app, or channel.

Sexualised attention toward contributors and "just asking questions" about trans bodies are both
on the wrong side of the line.

---

## Reporting

Email **rights@lumis.moe**. You do not need to be a contributor, and you do not need to write it
formally — a paragraph is enough. Tell us what happened and where, and include links or screenshots
if you have them.

We will:

- Read it ourselves. Reports go to the maintainers only; we do not forward them to a third party.
- Reply within a week. We are two students with no formal panel and no SLA — if a week passes,
  that is a reason to write again, not a reason to give up.
- Not ask you to confront the person you're reporting.
- Not publish your name, your report, or the outcome in a way that identifies you.

We can: warn, edit or delete comments, undo a merge, reject a contribution, revoke collaborator
access, remove a project from the sponsor's organisation, and ban. We usually start with the
smallest thing that makes the space safe again, and we tell the person what they did that counts.
We do not promise to tell you what we decided about someone else — we'll tell you it's handled and
you may not have closure and that is unfair but it protects the reporter.

If a maintainer is the problem: email the other one, or write to **Loners can Make Software**
(the nonprofit behind this project) at the address on its site.

## Enforcement against the project itself

If you think rights.wtf's *content* is dangerous to you — not wrong wording, actually dangerous —
say so in an issue labelled with the correction form and put `URGENT` in the title. Content problems
are fixed by changing content, fast, and we treat them with the same seriousness as conduct problems.

This site is not a crisis service. If you are in immediate danger, use local emergency services or
a helpline on our directory for your country.
